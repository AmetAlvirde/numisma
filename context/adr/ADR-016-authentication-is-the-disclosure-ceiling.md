# Authentication is the disclosure ceiling

_Made during: MVI — the Fill Path surface (spec #285), grill decision G-D2 of
`~/Dev/notes/numisma/2026-08-11-fill-path-surface-grill.md`. The crossing it sanctions is built by
slices #286–#289; this ADR is slice #290 and lands in the SAME PR, on the precedent ADR-007's
third amendment set for its own timing: an ADR filed later leaves `main` carrying a decision
record that flatly misdescribes the wire, which is the failure ADR-007's FIRST amendment exists to
punish._
_Scope: product_
_Status: accepted — decided by the fund's operator in the 2026-08-11 grill (G-D2); the fields it
sanctions ship with it._

The hosted surface is **session-gated and single-tenant**. `getDashboard` verifies a Better Auth
session against the incoming request's headers before any anchor is read, and `auth.ts` disables
self-service signup at the server (`disableSignUp`), so the product admits exactly one seeded
account (ADR-008, ADR-011). **The browser was therefore never the exposure boundary**, and sizing
each new wire field against "what if someone sees this on the phone" was sizing it against a
reader who is, by construction, the operator who authored the fact.

**The decision: the projection row may carry whatever the signed-in operator needs.** A field
earns its place on the wire by being needed by a surface, not by clearing a per-field disclosure
argument about the browser.

This ADR argues **exactly one thing**, and the three limits it does NOT move are as load-bearing
as the one it does:

- **The wire ships CONCLUSIONS, not inputs.** Unchanged, and it is the rule slices #286–#289 were
  built under. Orders, lots and torn acts stop at `push/dca-block.ts`.
- **The `dca` branch is DATE-FREE.** Unchanged — see below.
- **The committed fixture carries shape without magnitudes.** Unchanged; `push/fixture-synthesis.ts`
  synthesizes every new figure, because this repository is public and the fixture is the one place
  payload shape is readable without a credential.

The ceiling governs **WHO may see a fact**. It says nothing about which facts the wire was
designed to carry, and it is not a licence to widen the payload with raw inputs.

## What crossed on it

Verified against the branch rather than against the spec's list. Inside the existing `dca` branch,
all optional, all omitted rather than defaulted:

- Per-rung state — `venueAxis`, `bookAxis`, `label`, `joinProvenance`, `orderPriceUsd`,
  `declaredPriceMismatch`, `resting` — and the quantities they were measured from
  (`placedQuantity`, `venueConsumedQuantity`, `bookedQuantity`, `venueFilledFraction`).
- The rung's own `id`, and the ladder's `planId`, so a route can resolve one ladder.
- `DcaWireFillFigures` — `deployedUsd`, `unitsAcquired`, `avgEntryUsd`, `waitingDeclaredUsd`,
  `waitingRestingUsd`.
- `orphanLots` — a count of recorded lots no declared rung explains.

**Two fields crossed that the spec's own text does not list.** Both were decided mid-build, both
are squarely this decision, and recording only the spec's list would leave this ADR describing a
narrower wire than the one that shipped:

- **`$.dca.tornActs` — a fund-level count of outstanding torn fill acts**, on the branch ROOT
  because `reconcileFillActs` pairs the whole durable log against the whole orders sidecar and the
  result belongs to no ladder in particular. **A count, never the acts:** a `TornFillAct` carries
  an `orderId`, an `eventId` and a second-granular `observedAt`, and the stamp alone would break
  the date invariant. The surface needs to know THAT recording is blocked; the repair happens at
  the desk. It is present at `0` deliberately — with no neighbouring field to mean "a
  reconciliation ran", absence has to mean "this row could not check".
- **`$.dca.positions[].rungs[].sizeUsd` — the declared per-rung size.** ADR-007's third amendment
  had rejected it in writing ("a capital figure with no phone-glance use"). That reasoning expired
  against §6.3's accessibility decision: the chart is `aria-hidden` because it is presentation,
  and its one irreplaceable content — the **convexity of the capital curve** — is generated by
  `convexityCaption` from per-rung declared size and from nothing else. A sum cannot reconstruct
  the ladder it was summed from, so the accessible substitute for the chart had no data source
  without this field. The marginal disclosure is the ladder's SHAPE: every rung's price already
  crossed at v4, and `waitingDeclaredUsd` is already the sum of these sizes, so neither the levels
  nor the total is new.

Both are DECLARED figures or counts of the fund's own records — the operator's intention rendered
back to the operator who authored it, which is precisely what the ceiling covers.

## The rejection this supersedes, quoted

The live contract file records, on `GlanceBlock.feedGap`:

> Rejected: `markAsOf?` on every `CompositionRow`. That ships a per-instrument
> observation timeline — materially closer to the `priceJourneys` D8 dropped on
> purpose — and discloses which instruments are actively traded.

**That rejection is recorded in three places, and a reader who finds one and stops will conclude
the decision is unchanged.** All three:

1. `apps/web/src/projection/contract.ts`, in the docstring on `GlanceBlock`'s `feedGap` block —
   the live contract file, and the copy quoted verbatim above.
2. **ADR-007's `glance` amendment** ("Amendment: a third `glance` branch, and the payload stops
   being a bare `Pick`"), under the paragraph *"Per-instrument mark dates were considered and
   rejected."*
3. `apps/web/src/push/projection-payload.test.ts`, in the docstring beside the payload's date
   invariant — D14's runtime guard that no ISO-date-shaped value appears anywhere in the payload
   outside `dashboard.summary.asOf`.

**Which half survives, and which is superseded.** The sentence does two different jobs:

- *"ships a per-instrument observation timeline — materially closer to the `priceJourneys` D8
  dropped on purpose"* — **this half SURVIVES, unamended.** It is the conclusions-not-inputs rule:
  the push computes `feedGap` on the machine and ships the conclusion. The Fill Path was built
  under exactly that rule, which is why a count crosses where a torn act does not.
- *"and discloses which instruments are actively traded"* — **this half is SUPERSEDED.** Discloses
  to whom? The clause never names a subject, and the only reader is the signed-in operator, who
  knows which instruments they trade. It was sized against a browser that was already behind a
  session gate.

**`markAsOf` is still not on the wire, and this ADR does not put it there.** Two independent
reasons hold it back with the ceiling removed: the surviving half above, and the date invariant.
The ceiling was only ever one of three constraints, which is the whole reason this supersession is
narrow enough to be safe.

## No per-field ADR-007 amendment rides with this increment

The per-field amendment ceremony is retired here, deliberately. Its job was to make each crossing
**deliberate and visible**; three mechanisms now do that at one line each, and they fire on
`pnpm typecheck` / `pnpm test` rather than on someone remembering to open a document:

- **The version pin, in two places.** `COMPOSITION_SNAPSHOT_SCHEMA_VERSION` in `contract.ts`, and
  `PINNED_SCHEMA_VERSION` in `orders-stay-off-the-wire.test.ts` whose docstring must be read
  before the number moves in either direction.
- **The compile-time allow-list.** `ProjectionKeyAllowList` names every permitted key of every
  type in the payload's transitive closure; an unlisted field fails typecheck by name
  (`ENGINE_GREW_A_KEY_THE_PROJECTION_ALLOW_LIST_DOES_NOT_NAME`).
- **The runtime allow-list.** `ALLOWED_KEY_PATHS` in `projection-payload.test.ts` asserts the
  derived payload's full recursive key-PATH set equals a checked-in list exactly — catching a
  value that carries a key its type never declared.

Both allow-lists grew for this increment **by enumeration, one line per field**, never loosened to
a prefix or a wildcard; the mutation checks that prove it are recorded in those files. The
marginal value an amendment would have added over them is prose — and prose that restates what an
allow-list already enumerates is the thing ADR-007's own first amendment shows goes stale without
anyone being told.

**This should be the last disclosure-shaped ADR of this class.** The class is: *a field derived
from data the fund already holds, about the fund's own declared intention or its own records,
rendered to the one signed-in operator.* For that class the pin plus the two allow-lists are
sufficient, and the answer is now settled rather than re-argued per field.

**What would falsify that** — any of these re-opens the ceiling and costs a new decision, not a
paste into an allow-list:

- A crossing that answers a **whole-fund capital question** rather than one declared ladder's
  progress. That class is still refused by name in `ORDERS_SYMBOLS` — `composeAvailableCapital`,
  `committedRungs`, `committedByReserve`, `pickRestingOrdersAsOf`, `formatAvailableCapital` — and
  it is the class ADR-007's blast radius was computed without.
- **A second operator or a second tenant.** ADR-011's single-account posture is this ADR's
  premise; the moment there are two logins, "the signed-in operator" stops naming the author of
  the facts on screen.
- **Any reachable surface that is not session-gated** — a share link, a public read view, an
  unauthenticated status page. The ceiling IS the gate; remove the gate and there is no ceiling.
- **Data leaving the gated boundary by another path** — an export, a digest email, a bot, a
  notification. Auth bounds a reader, not a copy.

**The honest cost of not amending, recorded because it is real:** ADR-007's third amendment now
contains two sentences that describe a narrower wire than exists — that `sizeUsd` "stays off the
wire" and that "the rungs ship as `priceUsd` alone". Those sentences are overtaken by this ADR and
are not edited by it (that edit is out of this increment's scope). A reader who finds them and not
this document reads a stale wire description, and the index row for ADR-016 is what has to prevent
that. If a fourth ADR-007 amendment is ever written for another reason, correcting those two
sentences rides along with it.

## It does not license dates onto the `dca` branch

**ADR-007's DATE-FREE invariant on the `dca` branch is unaffected**, and the branch keeps it. The
Fill Path's provenance footer says *"Fills recorded through …"*, and that date is
`recordedThrough` on `FillPathView` — **the anchor's own `asOf`, passed through verbatim** by
`composeFillPathPage`.
No clock is read on the page, no fill timestamp is on the wire to read, and the branch still
carries no date-shaped value of its own. The two tests that pin the invariant and the committed
fixture's anchor-dates-only rule are untouched.

That is the distinction the whole ADR turns on: **the ceiling is about WHO may see a fact, not
about which facts the wire carries.** A fill stamp is withheld because the branch is date-free and
because a stamp is an input rather than a conclusion — neither reason was ever about the browser,
so neither is relieved by removing the ceiling.

The ratchet's own amendment — what the off-the-wire test admitted, and on what terms — is
recorded in `orders-stay-off-the-wire.test.ts`'s header, where the next person to move the pin
will read it. It is deliberately not restated here.

## Considered options

- **Keep the ceiling below the tap and ship less (REJECTED).** The pre-existing posture: size each
  field against the browser and omit anything not strictly needed for a phone glance. It was
  rejected because the cost falls in a place nobody expected — with `sizeUsd` withheld there is no
  accessible substitute for an `aria-hidden` chart at all, so the narrow wire is paid for by the
  screen-reader user; with `tornActs` withheld the surface cannot say that recording is blocked,
  so it is paid for by the operator staring at a figure that has silently stopped updating. A
  ceiling that protects a reader who is the operator, at the cost of the operator, is not
  protecting anyone.
- **Amend ADR-007 per field, as before (REJECTED).** Honest and legible, and it is what the last
  three increments did. Rejected because the ceremony's product is a prose copy of an enumeration
  the two allow-lists already hold, and the copies drift — this ADR is itself the evidence, having
  had to overtake two sentences in the third amendment. The guards that fail a build beat the
  guards that fail a reading.
- **Take the ceiling as licence to ship inputs (REJECTED, and this is the sharpest one).** If auth
  is the boundary, why narrow the payload at all — why not ship the orders and the lots and let
  the reader fold them? Rejected because the two rules answer different threats. The ceiling
  answers *who is reading the browser*; conclusions-not-inputs answers *what a compromise of the
  projection database yields*, and that threat is untouched by any session gate. Slices #286–#289
  hold that line structurally: the wall stands at `push/dca-block.ts`, orders and lots go in,
  per-rung conclusions come out, and a test asserts `reconcileFillPath` is called in exactly one
  file.

## Consequences

- **What crosses is durable in a way the schema is not.** The push writes `composition_snapshot`
  with `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT (fund_id, as_of) DO UPDATE` — one row per anchor date, upsert-only;
  nothing in this repo deletes a row, and the daily push adds one every day.
- **The wall moved rather than fell.** It used to separate the orders sidecar from the push
  entirely; it now separates raw order rows from conclusions and stands at one file. Two symbols
  (`loadOrders`, `resolveOrdersPath`) were NARROWED out of `ORDERS_SYMBOLS` into
  `ADMITTED_ORDER_SYMBOLS` — which is asserted PRESENT, so a wire that silently stopped
  reconciling fails loudly instead of passing every negative scan.
- **The version range, not this ADR, is what made the crossing deployable without a window.**
  `MIN_SUPPORTED_SNAPSHOT_SCHEMA_VERSION` and `isSupportedSchemaVersion` let the reader ship first
  and accept both shapes; a v4 row renders honestly as day-zero absence.
- **The public repository's exposure is unchanged.** Every new figure is synthesized in the
  committed fixture; structure, states and counts stay verbatim.
- **ADR-007, ADR-011 and ADR-013 are not amended.** This ADR stands beside them: ADR-011's
  single-account posture is its premise, ADR-013's beside-the-log placement of orders is
  untouched, and ADR-007's payload doctrine is narrowed nowhere.

## The three SDP tests

- **Hard to reverse.** Not the code, and saying otherwise would be flattery: the fields are
  optional, `git revert` un-writes them, and ADR-007's own doctrine is that *a read-model schema
  change is a re-push, never a data migration*. What does not revert is the **data**. Every push
  writes one row per anchor date into a third-party managed database, upsert-only, and nothing
  deletes rows; `pnpm backfill` can overwrite every row in place at a narrower shape, and that
  reversal path is real, but it reaches only the LIVE row — the provider's backups, replicas and
  WAL retention are not addressable from this repo, and anything already read was already read. So
  the reversible object is the schema and the irreversible one is the disclosure, and the gap
  between them widens by one row a day. It also gets structurally harder with each surface built
  on the fields: the route, the caption and the banner all presuppose them.
- **Surprising without context.** A future reader who greps `markAsOf` finds a written rejection in
  a LIVE contract file, in a test docstring, and in a standing ADR amendment, and will reasonably
  conclude the wire is narrow by policy. The surprise is not merely that the rejection was
  overturned — it is that **only one of its two clauses was**, and by an argument about the READER
  rather than about the data, so the same sentence still forbids `markAsOf` today for reasons that
  never depended on the ceiling. The second surprise is the direction of the fix: the intuition
  that a narrower wire is a safer wire is wrong here, and `sizeUsd` is the proof — rejected three
  weeks earlier as having "no phone-glance use", and then required by the accessible substitute for
  a chart that a phone-glance argument had never considered.
- **A real trade-off, and the losing side stated.** What is given up is the property that a
  compromise of the projection database — a leaked read credential, a provider incident, a
  mis-scoped grant, a stolen session — yields only levels and totals. It now additionally yields
  **the ladder's shape**: how the operator distributed capital across their own declared levels,
  hence the convexity of the accumulation plan, plus a per-ladder execution record (how much
  deployed, how many units, at what average). The pairing argument ADR-007's first amendment used
  against `invalidationWatch` — positions plus the price you are forced out at is worth more than
  either alone — applies against this decision too: rung prices plus rung sizes is worth more than
  rung prices alone, and that is a genuine loss, not an accounting artifact. It is taken anyway on
  three counts: the price levels themselves already crossed at v4, so the marginal fact is the
  distribution rather than the ladder; the rungs are already resting at the venue, which sees them;
  and a DCA rung is a declaration to ACQUIRE below spot, which an adversary cannot trigger against
  the operator the way a stop level can. Bought in exchange: the operator stops opening the
  exchange to find out where the ladder is, the chart gets an accessible substitute, and a blocked
  recording state becomes visible instead of silent. That weighing is a **judgment**, not a proof
  — a future reader with a changed posture (see the falsifiers above) should re-take it rather
  than inherit it.

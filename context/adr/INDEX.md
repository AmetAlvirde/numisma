Both halves are the decision, and the second is what makes the first load-bearing: under lexicographic selection a `Date.parse`-able non-ISO stamp (`"08/10/2026"`) sorts under `"0"`, ahead of every ISO date in the file, and **silently selects the wrong record** — no throw, no warning, a plausible answer; and `Date.parse("2026-02-30")` SUCCEEDS by rolling over to March 2, so a shape-only check accepts a string that sorts as February and means March (the round trip rejects it while leaving a legitimate `"2026-01-31"` alone). The exemption never covered the round trip: `observedAt` is now round-tripped through `isIsoCalendarDate` on its date half and range-checked on its time half (`isObservedAtStamp`, `packages/engine/src/orders/records.ts:293`), the same defect class `effectiveAt` closes. Trade-off: a closed strict wire format vs a permissive normalizing reader — strict costs the operator an occasional rejected line reported as a corrupt-line skip, and buys a file where a date means one thing forever; chosen strict only because it is chosen before the first line exists. |
